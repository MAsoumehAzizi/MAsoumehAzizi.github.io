Introduction to Optimization · ISyE 524 · Team of 3

Lowest-cost vaccine distribution for a rural county

Problem

Early COVID-19 rollouts in rural areas were slowed by supply and demand mismatches across vaccine brands with different cold-chain needs. How should 3 depots ship Pfizer and Moderna doses to 10 clinics so that every clinic's demand is met at the lowest transport cost?

Approach

We formulated a multi-commodity transportation linear program with 60 decision variables (depot × clinic × vaccine) plus supply and demand constraints. We generated synthetic depot and clinic locations, supplies and demands. Cost per dose was distance × a cold-chain rate of $3.00/mile for Pfizer and $2.50/mile for Moderna. We implemented the model in Julia with JuMP and solved it with HiGHS, then re-solved with unmet-demand slack variables to test supply shortages from 0.5× to 1.5× baseline.

Results
  • $958,104 optimal cost, solved in 25 simplex iterations with all demand met.
  • 22 active shipments. Depots 1 and 3 carry nearly all volume, and Depot 2 ships only 620 doses because of its remote location.
  • Pfizer's higher cold-chain cost routes most Pfizer volume through Depot 3 (2,423 doses), while Depot 1 serves most Moderna.
  • All demand is still met down to about 0.7× baseline supply. At 0.5×, 72% of Pfizer and 82% of Moderna demand is met.

Industrial Data Analytics · ISyE 412 · Team of 4

When and where U.S. traffic accidents happen

Problem

Which times, weather conditions, road features and places drive the number and severity of U.S. traffic accidents?

Approach

Cleaned and engineered features on a 500,000-row sample of the 7.7 million-record Kaggle U.S. Accidents dataset (2016–2023). We added accident duration and time features, trimmed duration outliers at the 99th percentile, imputed missing values, and joined county population data. We built the analysis in Tableau as interactive dashboards: time-of-day and day-of-week heatmaps, state and county hotspot maps, yearly severity trends, weather breakdowns and accident maps by traffic-control type. We also built STL time-series forecasts in R for the 10 counties with the most accidents.

Results
  • 73% of accidents happen during the day, peaking on weekdays from 6:30–8:30 AM and 3:30–6:30 PM.
  • ~2 hrs of traffic delay per accident on average.
  • 2022 was the peak year, with about 308 accidents per day.
  • Most accidents happen in fair weather, but wind and heavy precipitation are linked to higher severity.
  • Accident counts track population at the state level. The forecast was about 6,700 accidents in Los Angeles County for 2023.

Statistical Quality Control · ISyE 512 · Team of 3

Fill-weight quality study of fruit snack packs

Problem

Welch's fruit snack packs are labeled 22.7 g net weight. Acting as quality engineers, we tested whether the fill process is in control, whether it can reliably meet the label claim, and whether the measurement system can be trusted.

Approach

Three operators weighed 40 packs on three scales of different resolution, in 20 subgroups of two. We built X̄ and R Shewhart charts with revised limits. We then added CUSUM (k = 0.5, h = 4.25), EWMA (λ = 0.05, L = 2.5) and moving-average charts to catch small shifts, and finished with process capability and gauge capability analysis.

Results
  • Shewhart charts showed the process in control. CUSUM and EWMA picked up a slight upward drift that the Shewhart charts missed.
  • Mean fill was 23.58 g with σ = 0.85 g, off-center from the 24.2 g spec midpoint.
  • Cpk = 0.36, so the process is not capable. Under the fitted normal model, about 15% of packs fall below the 22.7 g label weight.
  • A precision-to-tolerance ratio of 0.36 (target ≤ 0.1) showed the measurement system was not capable either. We recommended more precise gauges, recalibration and a standard operator procedure.

Introduction to Quality Engineering · ISyE 575 · Individual project

What makes a smartphone charge faster? A 2⁴ factorial experiment

Problem

Which everyday conditions control how fast a phone charges, and which combination of settings charges it fastest? I tested four factors: cable (USB-C vs. USB-A), power source (wall adapter vs. laptop), starting charge (10% vs. 70%) and background activity (off vs. on).

Approach

I designed a full 2⁴ factorial experiment on a Samsung Galaxy S23 FE with two replicates, for 32 randomized runs. The response was battery gain over 15 minutes. I controlled nuisance factors by using the same handset, cables and ports and by cooling the phone between runs. In Minitab I fit the full model and used ANOVA, Pareto and normal effect plots to reduce it to the significant terms. I then checked the residual diagnostics and validated the reduced model with confirmation runs.

Results
  • All 4 main effects and 3 two-way interactions were significant (p < 0.05). The model explains 97% of the variation (R² = 904/934).
  • Cable type and power source dominate. Their strong interaction means a USB-A cable on a laptop port is far slower than either change alone would suggest.
  • The best setting was USB-C, wall adapter, 10% starting charge and background off. The model predicted a 23.19% gain in 15 minutes, with a 95% prediction interval of 20.26–26.11%.
  • Three confirmation runs gave 23%, 23% and 24%, a mean of 23.33%. All fell inside the prediction interval, which validated the model.

Manufacturing Systems · ISyE 641 · Team project

Redesigning a congested job shop with Quick Response Manufacturing

Problem

A mixed-routing job shop with shared CNC machines was buried in queues. Jobs spent more time waiting than being machined, WIP kept rising, schedules were reshuffled by constant expedite requests, and on-time delivery was unreliable.

Approach

We used QRM principles and chose short-routing aluminum parts as the Focused Target Market Segment (FTMS). Their routings are simple and predictable, and they put heavy load on the CNC bottleneck. We designed a dedicated cell with its own CNC capacity, cross-trained operators and in-cell inspection. Work in the cell runs first-come, first-served, and capacity is sized for flow rather than maximum utilization. Manufacturing Critical-path Time (MCT) replaces local efficiency as the main metric. The plan includes a phased 22-week rollout, an ADKAR change-management strategy and a risk mitigation plan.

Targets
  • 40–50% reduction in MCT
  • ~50% reduction in WIP
  • ≥95% on-time delivery
  • Zero routine expediting

These are the project's design targets, to be validated through simulation and the pilot cell. They are not measured results.

Information Technology in Supply Chains · Team simulation

Running a manufacturing company in SAP: ERPsim Manufacturing game

The simulation

ERPsim, developed at HEC Montréal, has teams compete to run a muesli manufacturer entirely through a live SAP system. Each team makes and sells 12 products (6 recipes in 500 g and 1 kg sizes) to hypermarkets, grocery chains and independent grocers. Teams work within real constraints: 24,000 units per day of production capacity, lot sizes of 16,000–48,000, 8-hour setups, supplier lead times of 1–5 days, and storage and fixed costs.

What I did

Our team ran the full plan-to-cash cycle in SAP on every simulated day. We forecast demand, ran MRP, converted requisitions into purchase orders and planned orders into production, set prices and marketing spend for each channel, and moved stock to regional warehouses. We also weighed investments in extra capacity and setup-time reduction against their depreciation cost.

SAP transactions used
  • Plan Forecasting (MD61) and MRP run (MD01)
  • Buy Purchase orders (ME59N) and PO tracking (ZME2N)
  • Make Production order release (CO41) and production reports (ZCOOIS)
  • Sell Pricing (VK32), marketing spend (ZADS) and stock transfers (ZMB1B)
  • Review Financial statements (F.01), inventory (ZMB52), cash flow (ZFF7B) and market data (ZMARKET)

Decision Analysis · ISyE 516 · Team of 3

Graduate school or the job market? A decision model for new industrial engineers

Problem

After a bachelor's degree, an industrial engineering graduate can start a master's right away, go straight to work, or work while studying part-time. Which path is best over a five-year horizon once money, satisfaction and work-life balance are all counted?

Approach

We built an objectives hierarchy and a probabilistic decision tree using salary, tuition and outcome data from Payscale, BLS, NCES and UW–Madison. The model used $85K and $95K salaries for bachelor's and master's holders, $40K tuition and $30K part-time income. We compared expected monetary value, scored each path with a weighted multi-attribute utility function (financial 0.5, satisfaction 0.3, work-life balance 0.2), and ran sensitivity analysis on costs and probabilities.

Results
  • Going straight to work had the highest expected value at $426.6K, ahead of the hybrid path at $323.9K and grad school at $246.3K.
  • Work also had the highest overall utility at 0.60, ahead of grad school at 0.50 and hybrid at 0.44.
  • The work path stayed the best choice across the tested cost and probability ranges. The hybrid path wins only when the probabilities are very high, meaning strong performance, full employer support and no burnout.

Facility Design · Fall 2026 · In progress

Designing a cellular manufacturing plant for long-life LCD displays

Problem

A case-study LCD maker is moving to a new site and wants a world-class plant built on cellular manufacturing. The plant must assemble three display models (M860, M970 and M1080) across 12 workstations, meet today's demand of 102,000 units a year, and absorb three years of growth at 15–30% a year per model.

Approach

I'm forming cells from the product routings, then sizing machines, operators and shifts. The sizing accounts for setup and cycle times, scrap rates, operator efficiency and machine failures (MTBF). The design covers lot sizes and JIT flow, buffers and material handling, preventive maintenance, and the facility layout on a 10-acre site. I'm weighing setup reduction, SPC and TQM against their costs, and pricing the full design in one-time and yearly costs, including WIP inventory.

Design inputs so far
  • 16.8 / 28.2 / 33.2 min of process time per unit for the M860, M970 and M1080. Soldering and bonding are the heaviest steps.
  • Demand grows from 102,000 to about 180,700 units a year by year 3, an increase of 77%.
  • The mix shifts toward the M1080, the model with the most work content, so the processing load grows about 84%, faster than unit volume. This happens before scrap and setups are added. The cells have to be sized for that mix, not just for total units.
